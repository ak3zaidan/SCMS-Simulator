# Message and security defect register (adversarial verification, 2026-09-19)

An independent verifier reproduced the pycrate oracle end to end, wrote its own X.691 PER encoder, implemented RFC 6979 in Python and re-ran the legacy Python to capture fresh vectors. 11 findings; the first five were fixed in that workflow.

## Verification

### overall

I could not make the encoders emit wrong bytes. The hand-written UPER engine and BSM codec are correct against two independent implementations of the standard plus the crate's own pycrate oracle, which I reproduced by installing pycrate and compiling all 43 J2735 modules myself (235/235). The 1609.2 envelope's 93-byte overhead is confirmed by decomposing real SPDU octets against the documented derivation. The SCMS port reproduces the Python reference on 74 freshly captured vectors with zero mismatches, and the forward-only privacy guarantee holds exhaustively. Signatures are genuinely RFC 6979, confirmed against an outside implementation. What I did find is eleven defects, none of them a mis-encoding: one CRASH (Point::from_sec1 panics on any non-33-byte SEC 1 point, reachable from Real::import_public with peer key material, and the doc comment advertises exactly the case that panics); one SILENT DATA LOSS (BrakeAppliedStatus bits above the 5-bit string are truncated rather than refused, the single place the codec departs from its own refuse-never-guess rule); one AVAILABILITY defect (a received, unverified certificate's 16-bit iCert drives an unbounded hash-chain walk — 510 ms per CRL entry per SPDU measured, so a planned 10,000-entry CRL means ~85 minutes of CPU for one crafted message); two proved I-S1 divergences the acceptance test does not cover (signature malleability and off-curve key import); and one wrong line in the published envelope derivation (the PSID byte-count threshold is 256, not 128 — the encoder is right and the document is wrong). Nothing here invalidates results the simulator has already produced through the honest path; four of the five serious items are reached only by malformed or adversarial input, which is precisely the traffic this simulator exists to model.

### uper verdict

CORRECT — I could not break it. The hand-written encoder is byte-identical to two independent implementations on every vector I built: pycrate compiled from the real SAE J2735 2024-09 modules, and a PER encoder I wrote from X.691 unaligned rules, plus the crate's own 235-vector oracle which I reproduced end to end (235/235 byte-identical, both directions, 2 unmodelled-container round trips). Bit-level trace of a distinct Part I message confirms every field: 3-bit preamble (ext + 2 optionals), msgCnt 7, id 32, secMark 16, lat 31, long 32, elev 16, accuracy 32, transmission 3, speed 13, heading 15, angle 8, accelSet 48, brakes 15, size 22 = 290 bits, 293 total, 37 octets with 3 zero pad bits; lower bounds subtracted (lat 1307440000, long 1060099999 against the asymmetric -1799999999, angle 66, vert 177, yaw 31533); no octet alignment anywhere; ceil(log2(range)) widths exact; single-value ranges take zero bits (PathPrediction writes only its extension bit); extension bits emitted for BasicSafetyMessage, PathHistory, PathHistoryPoint, PathPrediction, VehicleSafetyExtensions, MessageFrame and the two extensible-size bit strings, and absent for BSMcoreData, PositionalAccuracy, AccelerationSet4Way, BrakeSystemStatus, VehicleSize and PartIIcontent — each checked against the module text.

### envelope verdict

CONFIRMED field for field, with one wrong line in the derivation. I decomposed a real 103-byte SPDU octet by octet: 03 protocolVersion, 81 content choice, 00 hashId, 40 SignedDataPayload preamble, 03/80/0a + 10 payload octets inner Ieee1609Dot2Data, 40 HeaderInfo preamble, 01 20 psid, 8 octets Time64, 80 + 8 octets digest signer, 80 80 + 32 + 32 signature = 2+1+1+3+1+2+8+9+66 = exactly 93 B, matching 04-models 9.1 line for line. Certificate signer measured at 87 + 144 B cert; generationLocation exactly 10 B. The one disagreement is the parenthetical 'ITS-AIDs >= 128 take 3': Psid ::= INTEGER (0..MAX) has lower bound zero so COER encodes it unsigned, and the measured threshold is 256 — the document (and the test's assertion message) is wrong, the encoder is right (finding 6).

### crypto port verdict

EXACT. I ran the legacy Python myself and captured fresh vectors rather than trusting the committed JSON, then compared 74 independently recomputed values against the Rust: 0 mismatches. That covers caterpillar a/p/ck/ek for four seeds (range-64, all-zero, all-0xff, all-nine), A and P coordinates, f1 and f2 at five (i,j) including (u32::MAX, u32::MAX), three full butterfly flows (B, Q, certified public key, device d and q, and d*G == certified), linkage_seed_next, linkage_seed_at, pre_linkage_value, twelve linkage values across four i-periods, and the CRL entry's two seeds and five match outcomes. I read the Rust tests rather than trusting their names — they do assert the pinned bytes. The forward-only property holds exhaustively, not just on samples: for an entry revoking from i=10, 0 of 200 (cert_i, cert_j) pairs below the revocation period match and 200 of 200 at or above it do.

### equivalence verdict

GENUINE, BUT INCOMPLETE — and I broke it three ways. The script is real evidence, not trivia: one backend-generic function, 300+ rendered steps over 12 events x 4 entities with a certificate-attachment cadence, five attacks per message, plus cold-cache P2PCD, hash-CRL and linkage-CRL rejection, an expired certificate and a public-key-only signing attempt, all compared as a sequence. But I constructed three divergences it does not cover: an ECDSA signature malleated to (r, n-s) verifies true under Real and false under Modeled (finding 2); off-curve key material is rejected by Real::import_public and accepted by Modeled::import_public (finding 3); and every post-quantum primitive is supported by Modeled and refused by Real (finding 8). I found no size divergence anywhere.

### signature determinism verdict

YES, RFC 6979 — verified externally, not taken on trust. I implemented RFC 6979 section 3.2 with HMAC-SHA256 in Python, re-derived the end-entity private scalar from the RNG seed the way keygen does (seed mod (n-1) plus 1), and reproduced the crate's 64 signature bytes exactly: b34c4c95...b32b1ae || e5734ee0...a72edf0. Signing the same digest twice gives identical bytes, and two independent backends on the same master seed produce the same signature. One consequence worth naming: the output matches the RAW branch, not the low-S branch — RustCrypto does not normalise s here — which is precisely what makes finding 2 (malleability) reachable.

### [CRITICAL] v2xw-sec /Users/ahmedzaidan/Developer/SCMS-Simulator/crates/v2xw-sec/src/ec.rs:131

**Problem.** Point::from_sec1 PANICS (aborts the process) on any SEC 1 encoding that is not exactly 33 bytes. `AffinePoint::from_bytes(EncodedPoint::from_bytes(bytes)?.as_bytes().into())` converts a &[u8] into &GenericArray<u8, U33>; generic-array asserts len == 33 and panics otherwise. PROVED with catch_unwind: Point::from_sec1(65-byte G uncompressed) -> PANIC; Point::from_sec1(&[0x00]) -> PANIC; Point::from_sec1(33-byte compressed) -> Ok. Real::import_public(uncompressed65) -> PANIC; Real::import_public(identity1) -> PANIC. Modeled::import_public of the same 65 bytes returns Err cleanly.

Add a unit test asserting Err (not panic) for lengths 0, 1, 32, 34, 64, 65, 66.

### [MAJOR] v2xw-sec /Users/ahmedzaidan/Developer/SCMS-Simulator/crates/v2xw-sec/src/crypto.rs:633

**Problem.** ECDSA signature malleability breaks invariant I-S1. Real::verify_prehashed accepts both (r, s) and (r, n-s) because p256's VerifyingKey::verify_prehash does not enforce low-S, while Modeled::verify_prehashed (crypto.rs:926) compares the token bytes for equality and therefore rejects the malleated form. ### [MAJOR] v2xw-sec /Users/ahmedzaidan/Developer/SCMS-Simulator/crates/v2xw-sec/src/crypto.rs:589

### [MAJOR] v2xw-sec /Users/ahmedzaidan/Developer/SCMS-Simulator/crates/v2xw-sec/src/linkage.rs:319

**Problem.** The linkage-CRL check does unbounded work driven by an unauthenticated field of a received certificate, before any signature is verified. envelope.rs:1332 (linkage_revocation) feeds the certificate's own iCert straight into CrlStore::revokes_linkage_at_period, which for each CRL entry calls CrlLinkageEntry::matches, which walks (cert_i - self.i) SHA-256 steps on two chains, for every j in 0..jmax. iCert is IValue ::= Uint16, so an attacker may claim 65535. verify_plan performs this check BEFORE the certificate's own signature is checked ('Revocation, before any signature work'), so the certificate need not be valid. MEASURED (release build): matches(cert_i=1000) 0.40 ms, matches(10000) 3.8 ms, matches(65535) 25.3 ms; the real path, revokes_linkage_at_period with the default jmax=20, takes 510 ms for ONE CRL entry on one received SPDU. 04-models.md 9.6 plans for ~10,000 linkage entries, which is ~85 minutes of CPU for a single crafted certificate. A simulation run would appear to hang.

**Fix.** Bound the walk. Carry a maximum forward distance on CrlLinkageEntry (or a store-wide cap, e.g. the certificate lifetime in i-periods -- CAMP issues 3 years = 156 periods) and return false immediately when cert_i - self.i exceeds it, since no legitimate certificate is that far ahead of a revocation. Also move the linkage check after the certificate-signature op in verify_plan, or at least after the cheap validity-period check, so unverified certificates cannot drive it. Add a regression test that matches(u16::MAX as u32, ..) completes in bounded time.

### [MINOR] v2xw-sec /Users/ahmedzaidan/Developer/SCMS-Simulator/crates/v2xw-sec/src/crypto.rs:788

**Problem.** The modelled signature token is a pure function of PUBLIC inputs: modeled_token = SHA-256(label || public_material || digest || counter). Nothing secret enters it, so anyone holding a peer's public key material and the signing digest can compute a token that Modeled::verify_prehashed accepts. Today the API prevents this (an imported public key has secret: None, so sign_prehashed returns PublicKeyOnly), but the property the card claims -- that a modelled verification answers yes exactly when a real one would -- rests on that guard rather than on the construction. The moment v2xw-threat (currently a 6-line stub) grows a forgery model, it will succeed in modelled mode and fail in real mode: a third I-S1 divergence.

**Fix.** Mix the key's secret seed into the token, e.g. modeled_token = SHA-256(label || SHA-256(secret_seed) || digest || counter) with the verifier storing that per-key commitment alongside public_material at keygen/import time. Sizes and outcomes are unchanged, forgery becomes infeasible for the same reason it is in real mode, and the equivalence claim stops depending on an API accident. If the current design is kept deliberately, say so explicitly in the modeled backend's card under limitations.

(a) A bound: CrlLinkageEntry gains `max_forward`, defaulting to the new DEFAULT_MAX_FORWARD_PERIODS = 156 — the CAMP end-entity lifetime, derived from 04-models.md §9.6's 3,120 = 20/week × 52 × 3 years, with a `with_max_forward` builder for longer-lived profiles. A new private `seeds_at` holds both the forward-only guard and the bound, so `matches` and the new `matches_any_index` cannot drift apart. (b) The redundant walk is hoisted: revokes_linkage_at_period called `matches` once per index, and the chain walk does not depend on j, so it repeated `delta` hashes jmax times for nothing — exactly the product the attacker was multiplying. `matches_any_index` walks once and then costs two AES blocks per index, which is also what a real verifier does, so this removes work rather than hiding cost (the module's stated objection to caching still holds). (c) The expensive linkage check moved behind the cheap validity-period check in envelope.rs verify_plan; the hash CRL stays first, since it is one SHA-256 and a set lookup. The ops vector is unchanged either way. Documented, including that a certificate both expired and revoked is now reported OutsideValidityPeriod.
