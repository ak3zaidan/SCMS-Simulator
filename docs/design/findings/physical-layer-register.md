# Physical layer defect register (adversarial validation, 2026-09-19)

Two independent validators re-derived the radio and mobility models from the published literature rather than trusting the crates' own tests. 28 findings, 6 high. The physics is exact where it was re-derived; the defects are in the state machines and the composition seams.

## Radio

### overall

The physics core of v2xw-radio is sound and, where I could re-derive it independently, exact. The state machines and the composition seam around it are where the defects are.

What holds up under independent re-derivation:
- The NIST PER model reproduces my from-scratch Python implementation to 5e-10 dB across all 24 SNR-at-10 %-PER cells and the 1 % column, and raw PER values to 1e-13 relative. The Eb/N0 form printed in 04-models.md 4.7 and the received-SNR form the crate implements are provably the same curves (agreement 1.8e-15 dB). No finding.
- Air time is exact integer arithmetic and matches hand computation at eleven (bytes, MCS) points including both boundaries; SERVICE, TAIL and the symbol ceiling are all right, and air_time and the PER exponent share one function so they cannot drift.
- Thermal noise is -104 dBm in 10 MHz, the noise figure is applied exactly once (only in noise_floor(), used only by sinr_windows), and the per-MCS sensitivities are the EN 302 663 Table 1 values verbatim with dynamic = static + 3 reproducing the one printed Table 2 row.
- The shadowing AR(1) coefficient matches exp(-d/D_corr) empirically at four lags over four (step, D_corr) combinations, with the right stationary sigma; the dual-slope law is continuous at the breakpoint to 2e-10 dB.
- EDCA's parameter table, AIFS formula and the no-ACK/no-retransmission/no-CW-doubling rule are exactly right, and the last of those is right by construction, not just by test.
- Determinism: zero std transcendentals, order-independent interference sums bit for bit under six permutations, per-frame keys that are pure functions of (seed, link, instant), and no RNG stream cache growth under LinkFrame keys (measured len() = 0 after 150,000 per-frame draws).

The five that matter:
1. The EN 302 571 / TS 103 175 idle-time bound is mis-parenthesised. Reading it as T_on*(4000*(CBR-CTH)/CBR - 1) reproduces both of the standard's worked examples to 0.01 % with CBR as a plain fraction; the crate is 1.5-1.8 % high and has built a whole CbrUnit parameter, a "the standard's equation is wrong" narrative and an "unexplained 2 % residual" limitation on top of the error. This is the finding I would fix first: it turns a documented disagreement with a standard into a clean agreement.
2. EdcaOcbMac strands every frame queued behind another in the same AC — three frames enqueued, one granted, two stuck for ever. It only shows up when more than one frame sits in a queue, i.e. under exactly the congestion the simulator exists to study.
3. Mac::cbr reads the meter at the last busy instant rather than at now, so a node that falls silent reports CBR = 1.0 permanently and every DCC model throttles it to delta_min. The cbr_at accessor the source comment points at does not exist, and nothing else is public.
4. The documented medium-tier stack double-counts the Sommer building term: 34 dB of extra loss on a two-wall link, measured.
5. Preamble capture is decided against an arrival set that finish_rx has already mutated, so the outcome depends on evaluation order and, for an engine driven by end-of-frame events, capture is effectively never evaluated against a shorter incumbent. That contradicts the crate's own I-R2 claim.

Every one of the five is reproducible from the harness I built; none is visible to the crate's 127 tests. SERVICE and TAIL: N_SERVICE = 16 and N_TAIL = 6 are added once, inside per::data_field_bits, and the SAME function feeds both air_time and the PER exponent, so the two can never drift apart. A zero-length PSDU still costs one data symbol (22 bits do not fit in zero symbols), which is right.

Symbol-count ceiling: div_ceil, verified at the exact boundary. 400 B at 6 Mbit/s is 3222/48 = 67.125 -> 68 symbols; 300 B at 3 Mbit/s is 2422/24 = 100.917 -> 101. The crate's own property test (payload time minus bits/rate lies in [0, 8 us)) is a correct statement of "one symbol of rounding and no more" and I re-derived it independently.

SIGNAL field: 24 bits, one 8 us symbol, always BPSK 1/2 whatever the DATA MCS is, and evaluated separately in PerModel::per and in the windowed success_probability. This matches EN 302 663 Annex C.3 and produces the small documented asymmetry (the QPSK/BPSK frame-curve shift is 2 mdB less than 10*log10(2)), which is a genuine signature that the SIGNAL field is not being evaluated at the DATA modulation.

No signal extension is added, correctly - the 6 us extension is 802.11g ERP-OFDM only, not 802.11a/p. air_time deliberately excludes SIFS and the AIFS, which belong to the MAC.

### edca verdict

MIXED - the parameter table and the no-retransmission rule are exactly right; the queue state machine strands frames.

Parameter table: EXACT against 04-models.md 4.3 / EN 302 663 Annex C.4.4 Tables C.4-C.6. CWmin 3/7/15/15 and CWmax 7/15/1023/1023 for VO/VI/BE/BK; AIFSN 2/3/6/9; aSlotTime 13 us, aSIFSTime 32 us, aCWmin 15, aCWmax 1023. UP mapping UP 1,2 -> BK; UP 0,3 -> BE; UP 4,5 -> VI; UP 6,7 -> VO.

AIFS = AIFSN * aSlotTime + aSIFSTime: CORRECT and computed in integer nanoseconds, reproducing the document's microsecond column exactly: VO 2*13+32 = 58, VI 3*13+32 = 71, BE 6*13+32 = 110, BK 9*13+32 = 149 us. The resume-after-idle path in on_cca uses the per-AC AIFS (aifs_of_index), so the four categories genuinely differ.

Backoff draw: uniform in [0, CWmin] inclusive via below(cw_min + 1), which is the standard's range. The draw is taken unconditionally on every enqueue, before the medium state is consulted, so the stream position cannot depend on channel timing — a deliberate and correct determinism choice, documented on the card.

Broadcast is never retransmitted and CW never doubles: CONFIRMED by exhaustive reading, not just by the crate's test. There is no retry path anywhere: on_tx_done does nothing but record the busy interval, TxGrant.attempt is the literal 1 at the single construction site, contention_window() returns ac.cw_min() with no state, nothing ever writes to NodeState::retransmissions, and no code path re-enqueues a popped SDU. The card pins use_acks to a range admitting only false. This matches EN 302 663 Annex C.4.2's note exactly — OCB has no group-addressed ACK, so the backoff procedure runs once.

Backoff freeze and internal collisions: correct. on_cca(Busy) clears resume_at on every AC, and 10 ms of busy medium consumes no slots; poll() scans from the highest AC downwards, counts every AC that is eligible in the same instant as an internal collision, and grants the highest — 04-models.md 4.3's rule.

FAIL 1 (high): a frame queued behind another in the same AC is never granted. enqueue arms the backoff only if none is armed; poll clears it after a grant and never re-arms. Three AC_VO frames enqueued together on an idle medium, polled every 1 ms for 200 ms, produced one grant and left two frames stranded with backoff = None. Under a 10 Hz generator that never queues two frames at once the MAC looks fine (100 enqueues -> 100 grants), which is why 127 tests pass.

FAIL 2 (medium): poll() grants at ctx.now() rather than at the computed slot boundary, and the Mac trait offers no next_poll_at, so the transmission instant — and therefore the entire collision structure the medium tier depends on — is set by the engine's poll cadence rather than by the 13 us slot.

CBR meter arithmetic itself is sound: overlapping busy intervals are merged before counting so the ratio cannot exceed 1 (three overlapping receptions spanning 0-70 ms give exactly 0.7), the window prunes correctly, and the pre-first-window denominator is the elapsed time. The defect is the clock it is read at (see the Mac::cbr finding), not the meter.

### dcc verdict

MIXED - the adaptive control loop is a faithful implementation of TS 102 687 5.4 and converges as the standard intends; the EN 302 571 idle-time bound is mis-parenthesised.

Adaptive parameter table: EXACT against 04-models.md 6.1 / TS 102 687 Table 3. alpha 0.016, beta 0.0012, CBR_target 0.68, delta_max 0.03, delta_min 0.0006, G_max+ 0.0005, G_max- -0.00025, T_CBR 100 ms, update period 200 ms.

Control law: correct. The smoothing step CBR_ITS-S = 0.5*CBR_ITS-S + 0.5*(CBR_now + CBR_prev)/2 and the clamped leaky integrator delta = (1-alpha)*delta + clamp(beta*(target - CBR)) are implemented literally, the offset clamp is applied before the integrator (positive offsets capped at G_max+, negative at G_max-), and the result is clamped to [delta_min, delta_max]. I re-derived the fixed point analytically as delta* = clamp(beta*(0.68 - CBR)/alpha) = clamp(0.075*(0.68 - CBR)) and the crate reproduces it to 1e-6 at every load I stepped: CBR 0.1/0.2 -> 0.030000 (at delta_max), 0.3 -> 0.028500, 0.4 -> 0.021000, 0.5 -> 0.013500, 0.6 -> 0.006000, 0.65 -> 0.002250, 0.672 and above -> 0.000600 (at delta_min).

Duty cycle converges towards the 0.68 target: CONFIRMED. Closed-loop stepping (N nodes each transmitting at the allowed duty delta, CBR = min(1, N*delta), on_cbr every 100 ms to steady state) gives CBR 0.2914 at 10 nodes, 0.4708 at 30, 0.5564 at 60, 0.6120 at 120 — monotonically approaching 0.68 from below, with the equilibrium CBR = 0.68 - (alpha/beta)*delta = 0.68 - 13.33*delta bounded above by 0.672 once delta hits delta_min. That is exactly the behaviour the standard's anti-starvation floor produces and is the correct answer, not a defect. Under a rising open-loop ramp (CBR 0.05 to 0.93 over 12 s) delta falls smoothly 0.030000 -> 0.017476 with the loop's own 1/alpha = 62.5-update time constant, and the gatekeeper interval stays pinned at the 25 ms minimum until delta drops below T_on/25 ms.

Gatekeeper: t_go = t_pg + min(max(T_on/delta, 25 ms), 1 s) is implemented exactly as TS 102 687 Annex B states, including both clamps.

Reactive model: EXACT against Annex A Tables A.1 and A.2 — rates 10/5/2.5/2/1 Hz with T_off 100/200/400/500/1000 ms, and 20/10/5/4/1 Hz with 50/100/200/250/1000 ms; state bands < 30 / 30-39 / 40-49 / 50-60 (A.1) or 50-65 (A.2) / above, with the Active 3 upper edge inclusive and no floating-point edge error at 0.60 and 0.65. Hysteresis is zero by default and flagged todo-calibrate, as 04-models.md 6.2 requires.

J2945/1: the Rostami 2018 Table 1 values, the ITT ramp (100 ms up to B = 25, linear to 600 ms at 150 vehicles, capped) and the f(CBP) power ramp (20 dBm up to 0.50, linear to 10 dBm at 0.80) all match 04-models.md 6.4; the SUPRA step and the density smoothing are literal.

EN 302 571 floor enforcement: STRUCTURALLY CORRECT. Every one of the three models runs its own decision through the floor before returning (max of the algorithmic wait and floor.t_off), the unconditional 25 ms minimum is applied whether or not the load-dependent bound binds, the 3 % duty-cycle cap over a 1 s window is checked before a grant and delays rather than drops, the 0 < T_on <= 4 ms bound drops outright, and turning the floor off requires the explicit non-conformant En302571Floor::disabled(). Verified: with the floor on, t_off(1 ms, CBR 0.9) = 1000 ms and a 6.2 ms frame is refused; with it off, 0 ms and admitted.

FAIL (high): the VALUE of the load-dependent bound is wrong. See the first finding — the reading T_on*(4000*(CBR-CTH)/CBR - 1) reproduces TS 103 175 Table 2's 351.9 ms and 692.3 ms to 0.012 % and 0.005 % respectively with CBR as a plain fraction, while the crate's percentage reading gives 358.209 ms (+1.79 %) and 702.703 ms (+1.50 %). The crate's CbrUnit parameter, its "the printed bracket is negative for every CBR in (CTH, 1)" argument and its "unexplained 2 % residual" limitation are all artefacts of a misplaced parenthesis, not a defect in the standard.

### determinism verdict

MOSTLY PASS, with one real order dependence.

PASS - no std transcendental anywhere. Exhaustive grep of crates/v2xw-radio/src for f64::{sin,cos,tan,exp,ln,powf,atan2,hypot} and for method-form .sqrt()/.powi()/.exp()/.log10() returns only doc-comment prose and string literals inside ModelCard equations. The only direct third-party calls are numeric::erf/erfc -> libm::erf/erfc, which is the same pure-Rust crate v2xw_core::math re-exports, documented at numeric.rs:8-22. Everything else routes through v2xw_core::math.

PASS - interference sums are order-independent bit for bit. I permuted a five-interferer set (including two frames from the same NodeId, and overlapping/partial windows) through the full register_arrival -> sinr_windows -> success_probability -> finish_rx path in six permutations: the success probability was identical to the bit in all six (psr = 0.000010108580814042502), as was the ledger. numeric::sum_powers_mw sorts by (NodeId, value.total_cmp) before math::sum_ordered, so duplicate transmitters are also handled deterministically.

PASS - the same link and frame draw the same fading value regardless of evaluation order. (Fading, LinkFrame{link, frame}) is a pure function of (master_seed, domain, link, start-instant): re-drawing (3,9) at t=4_200_000 after 1,000 unrelated draws returned bit-identical -2.524133458199047. The crate's own forward/reverse receiver-order test is confirmed independently.

PASS - no unbounded RNG stream cache growth under per-frame keys. Measured RngRegistry::len() through TestCtx: 0 at start, 0 after 100,000 Fading LinkFrame draws, 0 after 50,000 plugin-domain LinkFrame draws inside finish_rx, and 1 after 1,000 Shadow draws on one long-lived Link key. EntityRef::LinkFrame is is_single_use, and RngRegistry::checkout derives-uses-drops without touching the shard map, exactly as documented.

PASS - no std HashMap reaches an ordering. arrivals, transmissions, tx_intervals, nodes, shadows, links, duty and the ledger maps are all BTreeMap; the only Vec iteration that reaches a float reduction is sorted first.

FAIL - preamble capture is order-dependent (finding 5). finish_rx removes the arrival from self.arrivals before evaluate() runs, so preamble_locked() sees a different incumbent set depending on which arrival is finished first. One identical scenario yields Lost(PreambleMissed) or Lost(Collision) purely on evaluation order. This violates the crate's own invariant I-R2 claim in lib.rs ("one model's draws never depend on ... the order receivers were evaluated in") for the high tier, and it perturbs the I-R3 loss-cause ledger. The Bernoulli draw itself is order-independent; the decision that precedes it is not.

NOTE (not a defect) - the AR(1) shadowing draw comes from the cached long-lived (Shadow, Link) stream, so its value depends on how many times loss_db has been called for that link. That is stateful by design and deterministic for a fixed schedule, but it means a link's shadowing is NOT reproducible from (seed, link, time) alone the way fading is. Tr37885 draws 1 or 2 values per call from the SAME (Shadow, Link) stream depending on whether the 100 ms restate interval elapsed, which couples the shadowing sequence to the restate schedule. Both are documented; neither is order-dependent across models, because only one Propagation model is active per run.

### [HIGH]  /Users/ahmedzaidan/Developer/SCMS-Simulator/crates/v2xw-radio/src/dcc.rs:118

**Problem.** En302571Floor::t_off_limit mis-parenthesises the TS 103 175 / EN 302 571 idle-time bound. The crate reads 4000*(CBR-CTH)/(CBR-1), finds it negative for CBR in (CTH,1), invents a CbrUnit::Percent/Fraction parameter to rescue it, and records an 'unexplained 2 % residual' against the standard's worked examples. ## Mobility and net

### overall

Both crates are well built and the headline claims mostly survive adversarial testing. The Jacobi determinism claim holds bit-for-bit in four configurations that go beyond the crate's own test, including MOBIL's RNG draw, the intersection models, Poisson demand and dynamic rerouting. The IDM equation is exactly right (48 hand-derived states, zero error) with the product form in the s-star denominator. Routing is clean against two independent oracles across all 4,624 pairs of a grid with banned turns. Every WSMP and GeoNetworking header size reproduces its cited composition arithmetically, neither layer fragments, and the loss-amplification formula genuinely uses per-fragment probabilities. Fourteen findings, none catastrophic but one that materially distorts every scenario. The one to fix first is `insert_trip`'s `gap.min(behind)` (should be `max`): any vehicle anywhere on the origin lane blocks every further insertion on it, which dropped 42.7 % of routed trips in a 600 s default-demand grid run — a silent, scenario-wide demand suppressor that no existing test would catch, because the crate's own spawn tests all start from empty lanes. Next are three model-fidelity issues: the medium-tier default IDM leaves `s*` unfloored and so brakes when the leader is 5-6 m/s faster (confirmed through the engine, not just the equation); the Gauss-Markov GNSS forgets the environment scale in the bias half of its confidence ellipse, so its '95 %' circle holds 68 % of its own errors in a deep canyon; and `NativeMobility::legacy()` spawns Kesting-2010 driver profiles, so the legacy-parity configuration cannot reach parity. Two reporting defects would bite a downstream consumer quietly: a commanded `Spawn` never appears in `MobilityUpdate::spawned`, and a duplicate fragment arriving after its set completed is recorded as a reassembly loss for an SDU that was delivered — which pollutes exactly the metric §7.4 exists to expose. The fundamental diagram's four numbers are real and in band and I reproduced all of them from the bins, but the capacity drop measures flows at two different densities (the code's own comment says it should not) and the capacity band is too wide to fail; the free branch above the critical density is also still decaying inside the measurement window. The remaining findings are documentation and hygiene: the MOBIL card states the asymmetric bias with the wrong sign relative to its own (correct) code, the engine duplicates the cooldown rule as a literal 5.0, MOBIL scores a speed-capped vehicle with an uncapped driver, `ByteLedger::merge` can double-count past the I-N1 witness, and the unattributed-drop guard is a debug-only assertion in a crate whose runs are release builds. All findings are reproduced by tests; the temporary test files have been removed and both crates are back to 174 + 83 passing tests, clippy clean and `cargo fmt --check` clean.

### idm verdict

The equation is right; the shipped default carries one behavioural artefact. I re-derived the acceleration independently (plain multiplications plus IEEE-754 `sqrt`, no library) for six states — free road, slower leader, stopped behind a queue, crawling into a queue, closing fast, and a leader pulling away — across all four presets and both the car and truck columns: 48 states, every one matching the crate EXACTLY (zero absolute error, not a tolerance). The `s*` square-root term is `2*sqrt(a_max*b)`, confirmed differentially: substituting `2*sqrt(a)+2*sqrt(b)`, `2*sqrt(a+b)`, `2*a*b`, `2*b` or `2*sqrt(b)` all move the answer by more than 1e-6 at an unclamped state, and only the product form reproduces the crate. Limits are exact: `a_max` at rest on a free road, exactly 0 at v0, exactly 0 standing at `s0` behind a standing leader, for every preset and class. The deceleration clamp is applied where the module documents it — after the enhancement, on the raw result, as `clamp(a_min, a_max)` — and saturates at exactly -6.0 m/s^2 at a 0.2 m gap and 30 m/s. What the module documentation understates: three of the four 'legacy' clamps (a_min -6.0, v0 floor 0.1 m/s, gap floor 0.5 m) are in force for Treiber2000, Kesting2010 and Kesting2007 too, not only for `legacy`; they are on the card, but a reader of 'the legacy clamps' would not expect them on the published presets. The one that differs by preset, `clamp_s_star`, is the defect: see the medium finding — the published presets leave `s*` unfloored, which makes the medium-tier default brake when the leader is more than about 5-6 m/s faster, and that reaches the integrated engine (5.000 -> 4.966 m/s in one step where a free road gives 5.140).

### jacobi determinism

HOLDS — verified empirically and bit-for-bit, well beyond the crate's own test. I compared full traces (pos/vel/acc/heading/yaw/t and LanePos, every field by `to_bits()`, every step) between a forward run and one with `EngineParams::reverse_order = true`, in four configurations the crate's `the_jacobi_update_is_order_independent` does not cover: (a) 30 vehicles on a 600 m single-lane ring, 400 steps, no lane changes — identical; (b) 44 vehicles on an 800 m two-lane ring with MOBIL enabled, 400 steps — identical, so the per-actor `RngDomain::LaneChange` draw does not shift when the walk is reversed; (c) the same with `IntersectionMode::SignalsAndGapAcceptance`, so the claims pass and the conflict resolution run — identical; (d) a two-lane signalised grid with `PoissonDemand`, lane changes, signals, gap acceptance and dynamic rerouting, 600 steps, ending with 41 actors on the road — states, spawn list and despawn list all identical. The design reason holds on inspection too: pass 3 freezes an `ActorSnapshot` sorted by `(arc length, actor id)`; passes 4-6 read only the snapshot and buffer every result; pass 7 mutates each actor only from its own buffered `Decision`; every container that reaches an output is a `BTreeMap`/`BTreeSet` or is explicitly sorted. Supporting checks: zero std transcendental calls and zero `f64::sqrt` in either crate's `src` (everything goes through `v2xw_core::math`); zero `HashMap`/`HashSet`; `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` in both; every `RngRegistry::ephemeral` call site (the documented stream-restart trap) is inside a `#[cfg(test)]` module. One further determinism contract I checked and found sound: `PoissonDemand::spawns_in` produced byte-identical trip lists for 100 ms, 1 s and 7 s windows over the same 60 s horizon (137 trips each, `seq` equal to the stream position).

### routing verdict

CLEAN — nothing found. I checked `Dijkstra::search` exhaustively against two independent oracles on a 3x3 grid with every left turn banned (68 usable lanes, 32 connections banned): a BFS reachability oracle and a Bellman-Ford shortest-path oracle, both written from the world's own connection graph with the same class, lane-kind and U-turn filters. All 4,624 ordered pairs agreed: 1,640 routes found and 2,984 refused, with zero false positives (a route to an unreachable lane) and zero false negatives (a refusal for a reachable one). For every route found, every consecutive pair was a permitted, non-U-turn movement onto a motorised lane admitting the class; the reported `cost_s` matched Bellman-Ford to 1e-9 and the sum of the lanes' own costs to 1e-6. Banned turns: never crossed. Sidewalks: refused as an intermediate lane, as an origin and as a destination, both when honestly tagged `LaneKind::Sidewalk`/`ClassMask::PEDESTRIAN` and when the mask lies (`ClassMask::ALL`) — the second `is_motorised()` filter catches the mis-tag, and a pedestrian router can still use the same lane. Closed lanes (cost `None`): never appear in any route and are refused at both endpoints. One-way against the flow: cutting one connection of a 6-segment one-way ring makes the downstream lane reachable from nothing but itself, and the router refused it from all five other lanes while still routing the long way round the remaining path (exact lane sequence l3-l4-l5-l0-l1-l2). Disconnected components: two cuts split the ring into {0,1,2} and {3,4,5}; all four cross-component pairs refused, intra-component routing unaffected. The frontier ordering (`total_cmp` on cost, then lane id, both reversed for the max-heap) is a total order, so equal-cost ties are resolved by lane id rather than heap luck.

### fundamental diagram independent

I re-ran the full default configuration (1 km ring, 16 densities from 5 to 140 veh/km, both branches, 5 min warm-up + 5 min measurement at 100 ms, 160 one-minute bins) and re-derived every headline number from `result.bins` with my own least squares rather than reading the reported fields. All six agreed to within 1e-6: capacity 1736.5 veh/h at a critical density of 25.0 veh/km; dynamic capacity 1432.0 veh/h over 55 congested bins; capacity drop 17.5 %; wave speed -12.90 km/h; jam density 147.7 veh/km (intercept 1905.7). Against §2.9: capacity in the 300-2,200 veh/h band (yes); jam density 147.7 against 'about 140', +5.5 % (inside the stated 20 % reading); wave speed -12.90 against 'about -15', -14.0 % (inside); capacity drop 17.5 % in the 5-20 % band (yes). So the reported numbers are real and all four bands are genuinely met — the crate did not fudge them. My judgement is nonetheless that two of the four checks are weaker than they look. (1) The capacity drop is not the same-density hysteresis §2.9 defines: it compares the free branch's peak at k = 25 with the jammed branch's peak at k = 30, and the five jammed bins that exist AT k = 25 are excluded by a strict `>`. Compared at one density the drop is 3.3 % (max-to-max) or 4.4 % (mean-to-mean) at k = 25 — below the band — and 15.4 % / 11.8 % at k = 30. That is the medium finding. (2) The capacity band, 300-2,200 veh/h, is FHWA's uncongested-regime range rather than a capacity target, and is wide enough that nothing plausible can fail it; the falsifiable comparison, measured 1,736.5 against the theoretical Q_max of 1,988.7 the same result carries (-12.7 %), is not tested. Two further observations: the free branch above the critical density is still breaking down inside the measurement window (-8.4 % at k = 30, -12.3 % at k = 35, -10.6 % at k = 50 from the first to the last of its five bins), so 'the free branch' is a transient there and the fitted numbers depend on which bins land in the window; and §2.9's convective-stability threshold (1,050 veh/h) is carried as a constant that nothing reads, while the time-mean/space-mean speed row is not carried at all — yet both cards claim `literature-checked` against §2.9. Reproducibility is solid: two runs of the same parameters gave identical bins.

### net verdict

The standards work is correct; the three issues are in accounting and reassembly bookkeeping, not in the sizes. HEADERS (§7.1, §7.2): I recomputed the 1609.3 p-encoding from the prefix/offset rule rather than from the crate's table and it agrees on every boundary, topping out at 0x1020407F = 270,549,119 exactly as the PSID tutorial gives. The WSMP header was checked exhaustively over 4 extension counts x 5 PSID widths x 6 payload sizes x the LLC/SNAP flag (240 combinations) against `1 + 3k + 1 + p(PSID) + p(len) [+8]`: all exact, and the three documented totals fall out — 4 with a one-octet length, 5 for a BSM with PSID 0x20 at >= 128 B (the length field flips at exactly 128, which is the only rule producing both documented anchors), 14 with all three extensions.
